# Hyperlocal rental and commerce marketplace — business plan
Prepared for Vivek Singh | 20 October 2026
Initial market: Mohali / SAS Nagar, Kharar, Chandigarh and Zirakpur
Status: researched strategy and planning model; customer demand, supplier commitments and pricing remain unvalidated.

## 1. Executive decision
Build a neighbourhood marketplace where people can rent useful things, book cars from approved operators, buy secondhand goods and purchase locally made products. Position it around reliable nearby transactions: accurate availability, clear total prices, documented handovers and a reachable support team.

Launch geographically narrow and operationally curated. Prioritise car rentals for revenue, with a small selection of non-car rentals and used essentials for local relevance. Pilot makers after the first transaction flow works. Design all three transaction types now, but release by readiness.

The commercial hypothesis is that local customers will pay or remain on-platform for dependable fulfilment and dispute support. Being hyperlocal or combining categories is not sufficient differentiation.

Assumptions: asset-light business; Android first; founder-led product work; no purchased vehicle fleet, warehouse or employed delivery fleet; 18+ transacting users; self-drive cars initially, with chauffeur services treated as a future separate category. Available budget and team are unknown.

## 2. Market evidence and uncertainties
Zoomcar and Revv have Chandigarh rental pages [1,2]. OLX has Chandigarh furniture listings [3]. These establish visible competition and supply, not market size, booking volumes or profitability. Operator websites also advertise service in the region; their actual availability, documentation and quality need direct verification.

Mohali and SAS Nagar must not be counted as separate city markets. SAS Nagar is also the district name; its administration includes Mohali, Kharar and Dera Bassi tehsils [4]. Keep user-facing neighbourhood names separate from legal jurisdiction and district records.

Alternatives include national rental platforms, local operators, OLX, neighbourhood groups and makers selling directly through social channels. Interview buyers about their last real transaction and sellers about their actual inventory and cancellations. Do not assume these channels are failing simply because they lack a unified app.

| Segment | Customer job | Proposed reason to choose this app | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Working residents / visitors | Book an available car for specific dates | Comparable all-in quotes, precise pickup and responsive support | Paid bookings at viable commission |
| Students / PG residents | Set up or clear a room affordably | Nearby essentials, inspection and organised pickup | Completed transactions and acquisition cost |
| Small event organisers / creators | Use equipment briefly | Availability calendar and documented return | Repeat bookings, low damage expense |
| Households | Sell idle goods or rent occasional equipment | Faster local matching with accountability | Time-to-sale and willingness to pay |
| Local makers | Sell gifts, art or custom products | Local discovery, clear lead times and order tracking | Paid orders and on-time completion |

Start with books, desks, chairs, simple household items, selected projectors / speakers from professional owners and a tightly vetted car supply. High-value phones, luxury goods and expensive cameras add fraud and inspection complexity; defer them. Defer food, cosmetics, medicines, safety-critical goods and regulated products until category-specific compliance is ready. Prohibit illegal, stolen, counterfeit and recalled goods.

No defensible local TAM has been established. Use a bottom-up model: verified supply × available capacity × achieved utilisation × transaction price × retained commission. Do not multiply the region's population by an assumed annual spend and call it validated demand.

## 3. Geography and launch sequence
Treat the region as one commercial cluster with multiple service zones and jurisdictions.

| Stage | Service footprint | Launch condition |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot | One selected 3–5 km catchment in Mohali or Kharar | Founder can reach suppliers and supervise fulfilment |
| Adjacent growth | Nearby Mohali / Kharar neighbourhoods | First zone meets fulfilment and economics gates |
| Regional rollout | Selected Chandigarh sectors and Zirakpur neighbourhoods | Verified suppliers, boundary rules and support coverage |
| Next city | One adjacent cluster selected from real demand | Repeatable local operating playbook and dedicated owner |

Do not assume all of Mohali–Kharar fits into a 5 km catchment. Choose the initial polygon after mapping supply and customer interviews. Cars can have a wider pickup catchment than ordinary goods. Display pickup distance and delivery charges, rather than pretending everything is equally nearby.

Begin with 5–10 vetted car operators offering a target of 20–40 truly bookable cars; 10–15 non-car suppliers with 40–60 rentable assets; 75–100 fresh used listings; and a later maker pilot of 10 sellers with 30–50 products. These are recruitment targets, not observed local supply.

A smaller truthful catalogue beats a large catalogue of unavailable items. Record the last availability confirmation, expire stale listings and track vehicles across operators to catch duplicates.

## 4. Commercial structure and value proposition
Operate an asset-light marketplace. Sellers and rental operators own their inventory; the platform provides discovery, workflow, payment coordination and customer support. Identify the contracting supplier clearly on each transaction. Marketplace status does not automatically eliminate liability.

Offer three distinct modes:
- Rent: dates, availability, deposit, handover, return and claims.
- Buy used: condition, offers, inspection, pickup and completion.
- Buy local: product variants, stock or production lead time, fulfilment and returns.

Within Rent, cars need their own eligibility, documentation, pricing and emergency flows. Do not put them through a generic listing checkout.

A useful brand promise: “Useful things nearby. Clear prices. People you can reach.” Avoid unqualified claims such as “100% safe”, “fully insured” or “guaranteed earnings”.

Why sellers stay: incremental demand, less coordination, payment records, repeat customers and documented claims. Why buyers stay: reliable availability, clear price, pickup evidence, dispute handling and booking history. Test these benefits before expecting users to accept a commission.

## 5. Car rental operating model — a launch gate
Begin with established operators whose authority to rent each vehicle and insurance coverage have been checked. Do not activate arbitrary private white-plate cars merely because the owner has an RC and ordinary insurance.

The Rent-a-Cab framework is listed by transport authorities [5]. Public historical texts alone do not establish the currently applicable Punjab / Chandigarh implementation, amendments, or the platform's own licensing position. Obtain a written local transport-law assessment and written insurer confirmation before activating this category. This research does not establish that private peer-to-peer car renting is authorised in the proposed model.

Approval checklist:
- Operator legal identity, bank account, applicable licences and contracting authority.
- Vehicle ownership or valid leasing authority; registration, permit and fitness requirements as applicable.
- Insurance explicitly covering the proposed self-drive rental use, authorised drivers and destinations.
- Policy excess, theft exclusions, prohibited use, claim reporting and liability allocation.
- Current PUC and relevant tax / document status.
- Driver licence validity and class; eligibility aligned with operator and insurer requirements.
- Written rules for interstate trips, airport delivery, breakdowns and emergency escalation.
- Marketplace / aggregator obligations assessed independently of supplier licensing.
- Document expiry dates that automatically pause affected inventory.

Do not assume a yellow plate alone proves self-drive eligibility. Do not add chauffeur services under the same policy without a separate review.

### Car booking workflow
Search by dates and pickup → eligibility screening → exact quote and operator confirmation → payment through approved provider flow → signed rental terms → pickup inspection and OTP → active booking support → return inspection → claim review if needed → deposit refund and settlement.

Initially use request-to-book. Only enable instant booking for operators with reliable inventory synchronisation. Show confirmation deadlines and what happens if the operator declines.

Every quote must distinguish base rent, duration, included kilometres, extra-kilometre rate, fuel policy, delivery, tolls / taxes, refundable deposit, cancellation charges and late-return charges. Show applicable taxes and total payable before confirmation.

Pickup evidence: all exterior sides, wheels, windscreen, cabin, fuel and odometer; timestamp; existing defects; accessories; both parties' acknowledgement. Return uses the same checklist. Encrypt evidence and make access auditable.

Define ordinary wear versus chargeable damage. Owners cannot deduct deposits just by submitting an allegation. Give the renter the evidence, an opportunity to respond, repair substantiation and an appeal path. State a short claim-submission window with exceptions required by law or insurer obligations. Return undisputed amounts promptly within provider-supported timelines.

A deposit is not insurance and may be far smaller than a vehicle loss. Decide who pays excess, uninsured damage, towing, downtime and third-party claims. Do not finance uncapped guarantees from commissions. Never market a platform protection fee as insurance without the appropriate arrangement.

Have a real after-hours emergency route for active rentals. Cap operating hours or booking scope if coverage is not available. Operators need backup-vehicle and stranded-customer procedures; the app should never promise a replacement it cannot supply.

## 6. Other rentals, resale and makers
### Non-car rentals
Use item-level serial numbers where relevant, replacement-value bands, condition grading, accessories lists and availability buffers. Start with manageable-value inventory and professional suppliers. Define cleaning, late return, consumables, extension requests and loss responsibility.

If an asset can be rented and sold, reserve it across both modes. Disable sale while an active or future booking prevents transfer. After a sale, cancel availability rather than leaving an orphan rental listing.

### Used goods
Start with free listings, offers and organised local pickup. Distinguish:
1. Direct local deal: parties inspect and pay directly; no platform payment protection is implied.
2. Assisted transaction: supported checkout, handover record and explicitly scoped dispute service.

Only assisted transactions count toward commission-bearing GMV. Do not infer completion from a chat message or count an advertised price as revenue.

Require actual condition, known defects, ownership declaration and original photos. High-value categories need stronger proof and inspection later. Pickup OTP confirms handover, not waiver of rights for concealed defects or fraud. Do not promise a blanket “no returns” policy across all seller types and situations.

### Local makers
Begin with non-regulated gifts, art, decor and crafts. Each product specifies materials, dimensions, variants, ready stock versus made-to-order, production time and delivery date.

For custom work, capture the agreed design, approval step, milestones, changes, cancellation conditions and handling of defective or incorrect goods. Collect any advance through a provider-approved flow. Do not promise arbitrary multi-week holds without provider confirmation.

Separate casual sellers from businesses in onboarding and tax handling. Avoid treating all handmade items as exempt from product, consumer or tax obligations.

## 7. Trust, fraud and day-to-day operations
Use verification levels rather than one vague badge:
- Phone verified: confirms access to a number.
- Identity checked: specify what was checked and when.
- Business checked: business and bank ownership assessed.
- Vehicle documents checked: scoped to a particular vehicle and expiry.
None means guaranteed honesty or guaranteed insurance coverage.

Collect identity documents only when justified by category and risk. Prefer verified results and reference tokens where possible over retaining raw identity images. Do not demand Aadhaar for every low-value sale. Restrict exact residential addresses to appropriate transaction stages.

Common threats: fake payment screenshots, QR-code receive-money scams, advance-fee requests, duplicate listings, stolen goods, switched items, fabricated damage, referral collusion and payment reversal after payout.

Controls: payment confirmation from authenticated provider webhooks; transaction limits for new sellers; document review; payout-change re-verification; duplicate-image / asset checks; report and block; visible scam guidance; human appeals. Never collect a buyer's UPI PIN or OTP.

Operational targets, not legal promises:
- Availability confirmation within 15 minutes during published hours.
- Support acknowledgement within two business hours.
- Documented complaint decision target within three working days, with escalation when investigation takes longer.
- Refund initiation target within 48 hours after undisputed return acceptance, subject to contracted provider capability.
Publish customer-facing commitments only after staff and vendors can meet them.

Delivery: default to pickup; offer independently quoted third-party transport when practical. Record payer, distance, loading, stairs, waiting charges and who bears transit damage. Batch bulky-item pickups. Avoid subsidising every low-ticket delivery.

Minimum team for a live mixed marketplace: product / engineering owner, local supply-and-operations owner, scheduled support coverage, and retained accounting / legal expertise. One founder can run discovery; one founder cannot sustainably provide continuous car support, dispute handling and development without limits.

## 8. Pricing and unit economics
All prices below are experiments, not competitor quotes. Values are INR, before applicable GST. Model supplier commission deducted from the base transaction value; no additional buyer fee assumed.

| Category | Test price policy |
|---|---|
| Cars | 12–15% operator commission; model uses 15% |
| Other rentals | 15% commission |
| Makers | 12% commission |
| Used listings | Free basic listing |
| Assisted used sale | Optional 5%, minimum ₹99, maximum ₹299; disclose service scope |
| Delivery / inspection | Separately disclosed quote, no assumed profit |
| Supplier software plan | Later test ₹499–₹999/month once measurable demand exists |
| Sponsored placement | Later only, clearly labelled, with billing-policy review |

Do not charge for “verified” status in a way that lets sellers buy trust. Seller software subscriptions and boosts are digital features requiring a separate Play billing analysis [8].

### Illustrative contribution per completed transaction
| Metric | Car booking | Other rental | Maker order | Assisted resale |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Base transaction value | 6,000 | 1,000 | 1,200 | 3,000 |
| Commission | 900 | 150 | 144 | 150 |
| Payment cost allowance | 150 | 25 | 30 | 75 |
| Variable support / verification | 120 | 35 | 25 | 35 |
| Expected refund / fraud / claims allowance | 120 | 25 | 20 | 25 |
| Contribution before acquisition and fixed costs | 510 | 65 | 69 | 15 |

Payment cost is modelled at 2.5% of base GMV as a planning allowance, not a gateway quote. Obtain actual pricing, taxes, refund and settlement charges. Deposits, refundable security, delivery pass-throughs and tax collections are excluded from GMV and revenue here. If collecting a deposit incurs fees, include those additional fees in the model.

The claims allowance is an expected-cost placeholder, not insurance or a maximum loss. A single ₹20,000 uncovered loss consumes roughly 39 car bookings' contribution at these assumptions.

Resale is thin-margin even before acquisition. Free direct deals do not generate the modelled ₹15. If assisted resale does not cover costs, raise its service price, sell a valuable inspection service or keep resale as a low-support discovery channel.

Illustrative ₹1.20 lakh base monthly spend: local operations ₹35,000; scheduled support ₹20,000; infrastructure / tools ₹10,000; accounting / admin ₹10,000; founder allowance ₹15,000; acquisition ₹30,000.

At the base mix, contribution averages ₹151.11; about 795 completed transactions cover ₹1.20 lakh fixed costs if staffing and mix remain unchanged. Extra support needs can push the threshold higher. Cars contribute 75% of base-case contribution; this is not yet a diversified profit engine.

With a two-day car booking assumption, 120 bookings require 240 booked car-days. A pool of 30 cars would need eight days per car per month through this platform, before allowing for downtime and competing channels. At 300 bookings and 40 cars, that is 15 days per car. Validate actual calendar access, not just listings.

Acquisition example: ₹300 car-customer CAC can fit within ₹510 first-booking contribution. ₹150 CAC for a maker customer cannot be recovered from one ₹69 order; it needs repeat orders. Do not assume lifetime value without cohorts. Aggregate ad spend is already included in fixed cost; do not subtract CAC a second time in these scenarios.

## 9. Funding and cash discipline
First run a four-to-six-week concierge validation with a ₹30,000–₹60,000 planning cap for local research, simple tooling, travel and measured acquisition. This excludes full car legal activation, guarantees and a production app. If car clearance is not available, validate other categories and record non-binding car demand.

For an actual founder-built 90-day paid pilot, a planning envelope is:
- One-time product, legal and setup work: ₹1–₹1.5 lakh.
- Three months at the base operating budget: ₹3.6 lakh.
- Working-capital / contingency reserve: ₹75,000.
- Total before revenue receipts: ₹5.35–₹5.85 lakh.

These are budget allocations, not vendor estimates. Full outsourcing, meaningful engineering salaries, insurance premiums or a large car-claims exposure can require substantially more. Customer deposits and seller balances cannot fund operating expenses. Keep a weekly cash-flow view of provider receivables, refunds, seller liabilities and available company cash.

Release spending in stages: research → verified supply and compliance → working transactions → scaled acquisition. Do not buy a fleet to manufacture supply before demand is proven.

## 10. Go-to-market and validation
### First 30 days
Interview at least 30 prospective buyers across segments, 10 car operators, 10 goods suppliers / makers and five community gatekeepers. Ask about the last completed transaction, total price, failures, time spent, refund experience and willingness to use the proposed workflow.

Mystery-shop ten local car quotes using identical dates and vehicle class. Record final payable amount, deposit, kilometre allowance, fuel, pickup, interstate permission and cancellation policy. Do not publish unverifiable claims about competitors.

Recruit inventory with permission. Photograph and describe items accurately. Secure supplier agreements and a realistic availability-update routine.

### Acquisition channels
Use approved college / PG / society partnerships, local search landing pages, supplier shop QR codes, maker demonstrations, moving-day events and trackable referrals after completed transactions. Do not scrape private groups, auto-message members or pay for fake reviews.

Use the web to let people view listings before requiring an install. Android is the repeat-use channel. English and Hindi can lead; add Punjabi content based on actual use.

Lead with one customer job per campaign: “book a documented self-drive rental”, “set up your PG room”, or “buy a locally made gift”. A single generic “rent and buy everything” ad will be hard to optimise.

### Proposed release plan
| Period | Deliverable | Decision gate |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Interviews, supplier map, legal / payment feasibility | Evidence of real problems and reachable supply |
| Weeks 3–6 | Concierge pilot, manually reviewed transactions | 30 completed paid transactions across at least 20 distinct buyers in approved categories |
| Weeks 7–10 | Android closed beta, admin console, core integrations | Refunds, handovers and settlement tested end-to-end |
| Weeks 11–13 | Limited Play release in initial catchment | Reliable fulfilment and support; legal and store checks complete |
| Following 3 months | Improve retention and category economics | Adjacent zones only when operating gates hold |

Timings are conditional on team capacity and external approvals. Car approval is not assumed to fit the schedule. Keep car inventory unpublished until cleared; non-car launch can proceed if independently ready.

## 11. Product scope and customer experience
Bottom navigation: Explore, Post, Activity, Messages, Profile. Explore contains Rent / Buy used / Made nearby with a prominent car entry under Rent. Avoid three separate apps.

MVP:
- Browsing without login; phone login when transacting.
- Manual locality choice; optional foreground location.
- Category-specific listing forms and transparent condition / price.
- Date-based rental search and request-to-book.
- Offer / inspection flow for resale; stock / lead-time flow for makers.
- Payment, refunds, settlement records and supplier invoices as applicable.
- In-app messages, report / block and structured support tickets.
- Pickup / return OTP and evidence capture.
- Saved items, relevant notifications and completed-order reviews.
- Seller inventory calendar, away mode and payout visibility.
- Staff console for approvals, expired documents, disputes and reconciliation.

Defer auctions, wallet balances, lending / BNPL, nationwide shipping, proprietary delivery fleets, social feeds, AI chatbots and complex recommendations. A later “wanted nearby” feature can capture unmet demand without fabricating inventory.

Empty search results should offer a permitted nearby radius expansion or a consented availability alert. Do not show unavailable cars or fake activity to make the marketplace look busy.

## 12. Expandable architecture
Recommended design choice: React Native with TypeScript for Android, a responsive web catalogue / seller portal, and a modular backend with PostgreSQL plus PostGIS. A Flutter implementation is also workable; choose based on the actual builder's strengths. No commitment to a particular vendor or library version is required at this stage.

Start as one deployable backend with clear domain boundaries. Use object storage for evidence, a background job queue, push notifications and provider adapters. Avoid premature microservices.

Core domains:
1. Accounts, roles, consent and verification.
2. Regions, localities, legal jurisdictions and service zones.
3. Listings, categories, attributes, assets and inventory.
4. Rental reservations, sale orders and production orders.
5. Quotes, fees, taxes, payments and ledger entries.
6. Pickup, return, fulfilment and inspection evidence.
7. Disputes, moderation, reviews and support.
8. Notifications, reporting and staff audit trails.

Key entities: User, SupplierBusiness, Verification, Region, ServiceZone, Jurisdiction, Listing, InventoryUnit, Vehicle, AvailabilityBlock, Quote, Reservation, SaleOrder, ProductionMilestone, Inspection, Claim, Payment, Refund, Settlement, LedgerEntry, PolicyVersion and AuditEvent.

Store a shared listing identity, but typed details and workflows for each transaction mode. Important searchable fields should be typed columns; flexible category attributes can be versioned JSON. Avoid one giant unvalidated metadata object.

Geography rules:
- One regional cluster can contain several city names and jurisdictions.
- Serviceability depends on actual location, supplier radius, category and legal restrictions.
- Treat Mohali / SAS Nagar as aliases where appropriate.
- Preserve Punjab / Chandigarh and later Haryana distinctions for tax and transport rules.
- Store exact addresses privately and coarse discovery locations separately.
- City launch uses configuration, permissions and policies rather than hard-coded UI changes.
- Set category, price, commission and eligibility rules per region with effective dates.
- Snapshot accepted quote and terms so later policy edits cannot rewrite an existing order.

Critical engineering:
- Reserve time ranges atomically in the database, with maintenance / cleaning buffers.
- Expire unpaid holds and release them safely.
- Persist provider webhooks with signature checks and idempotency keys.
- Never mark paid from a client success screen alone.
- Use a double-entry internal ledger for fees, seller liabilities, refunds and deposits; it is an accounting record, not a customer wallet.
- Reconcile the ledger with provider statements daily.
- Separate payment, rental, fulfilment and dispute states.
- Maintain append-only histories for staff actions and financial adjustments.
- Enforce per-role access; require stronger controls for bank changes and large refunds.
- Use encryption, short-lived evidence URLs, backups and restore exercises.
- Queue notifications separately from booking confirmation.

Test the costly failures: two buyers reserve the same car; payment succeeds but response is lost; duplicate webhook arrives; supplier cancels after collection; refund succeeds but local acknowledgement fails; return evidence is missing; payout account changes; user requests deletion during a lawful retention period.

Example rental states: requested, awaiting confirmation, awaiting payment, confirmed, checked out, return pending, completed. Cancellation, expiry and dispute are explicit transitions or related states. Do not erase the booking when refunding it.

## 13. Legal, payment, privacy and Play readiness
### Consumer and marketplace obligations
Publish clear business and supplier identification, customer-support and grievance routes, all-in pricing, cancellation / refund rules and category-specific terms. Obtain a lawyer-reviewed responsibility matrix for platform, supplier, customer and courier.

The government announced e-commerce amendments effective 1 January 2027, including clearer sponsored listings, complaint handling, search transparency and dark-pattern controls [6]. Design for that launch horizon and verify the final applicable text. The source also describes NCH convergence participation and a 30-day prior-price basis for discount claims; have counsel translate these into operational requirements.

### Payments and tax
Use a provider-approved marketplace settlement product. Razorpay Route publicly describes split payments and vendor settlement features [9]; that does not establish that it will approve this exact model. Obtain written approval for individual sellers, rental deposits, delayed payouts, refunds, chargebacks and negative balances.

Do not call an ordinary current account “escrow” or manually build an unlicensed customer wallet. Do not promise UPI card-style authorisation holds. If deposit capture / refund is the supported method, model the cost and timelines.

Ask a CA to map each category and seller type: platform GST, seller GST, enrolment exceptions, GST TCS / reporting, applicable e-commerce income-tax withholding, invoice issuer, returns, credit notes and cross-jurisdiction supplies. GST's current GSTR-8 manual includes unregistered-supplier reporting [10]; this is not a blanket exemption for all sellers.

A short delivery from Punjab to Chandigarh can cross a tax boundary. Where a seller relies on an intra-state-only exemption, serviceability must enforce it. Casual personal-asset sales, regular resale businesses, makers and vehicle services require separate treatment. Confirm current law and rates at implementation; do not hard-code a universal tax rate.

### Privacy
DPDP Rules 2025 have staged commencement; the official notification defers many operational provisions for 18 months after publication [7]. Implement purpose-specific notices, data minimisation, access control, retention / deletion, processor contracts and incident handling now, with legal mapping to effective dates.

18+ trading is a proposed scope limit; car-driver rules may be stricter. Do not retain raw identity documents indefinitely. Deletion must explain narrowly justified legal retention without retaining everything forever. Avoid background location and unnecessary contact permissions.

### Play Store
Physical goods and rentals fall under the physical-transaction treatment in Google's payment policy; digital boosts and seller-app features need separate billing review [8]. Do not assume one exemption covers everything sold inside the app.

Provide report / block and ongoing moderation for listings, chat and reviews [11]. Provide account deletion in-app and a web request route [12]. Maintain a public privacy policy, accurate Data safety declarations and appropriate permissions. Verify current developer verification, testing and target API requirements at submission rather than relying on a fixed number in this plan.

## 14. Metrics, expansion and stop conditions
Primary measure: completed transactions with positive contribution and acceptable customer outcomes, tracked by category and zone.

Track searches with suitable availability; request-to-confirmation conversion; response times; cancellation rate; refunds; dispute cost / GMV; booked days per asset; listing time-to-sale; repeat transactions; paid versus organic acquisition; support minutes per order; and aged seller / customer liabilities.

Suggested expansion gates, to calibrate with pilot data:
- At least eight consecutive weeks of stable operations and 100+ completed transactions in the zone.
- At least 60% of qualified searches find three genuinely suitable options; report category differences.
- Supplier-caused cancellation below 5%.
- At least 90% on-time handovers.
- Positive contribution after variable costs in the core category.
- No unresolved backlog of overdue undisputed refunds.
- Measured acquisition payback within 90 days for the segment being scaled.
- Repeat use assessed by category and realistic purchase frequency, not one blended retention rate.
- A named person owns supply and support in the next zone.

Pause a category if legally viable supply cannot be secured, insurance exclusions are unacceptable, fraud exceeds the planned allowance, or support costs overwhelm commission. If users consistently move off-platform, investigate whether checkout provides sufficient value before restricting contact.

Do not expand geography and add several high-risk categories simultaneously. A configurable app makes expansion technically possible; operational discipline makes it economically possible.

## 15. First actions
1. Pick one pilot catchment and one revenue-leading category.
2. Conduct operator interviews and obtain written transport / insurance feasibility.
3. Get marketplace payment-provider approval and a category-wise tax map.
4. Recruit a small truthful catalogue and run concierge transactions.
5. Measure fulfilment, support cost and actual willingness to pay.
6. Build the Android and admin workflow around observed failures.
7. Release additional categories only after their operations work.

Recommendation: pursue the concept as a focused local business experiment. Use vetted car operators as the potential revenue engine, used essentials as a local discovery channel, and makers as a curated expansion. Prove dependable transactions before paying for mass app installs.
